# Safety: First priority

- Risk assessment: read this! (<https://linacreboat.club/>)
- Medics available at all times: find any marshal
- Weather might be cold: keep a lookout for hypothermia
  - shivering; pale, cold and dry skin; slurred speech; slow breathing; tiredness or confusion
  - move them indoors; dry clothing; contact medics
  - <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia/>

# Safety: First priority

# Getting ready to race

### The week before

- Decide warm-up plan, the start commands, and a race plan (with your coach).
- Check the boat safety
  - bow-ball, firmly attached
  - heel restraints/quick release: shoes don't come above vertical and can be undone with one hand.
  - boat lights, charged!
  - hatch covers, present!
  - backstays, present
  - Bolts and fittings, tight.

Lights are required if boating after 2:30pm each day. Anyone in the last scheduled block on Wed & Thu will need lights.

---

# Getting ready to race

### The night before

- Racing equipment (small bag of tools: adjustable spanner, screwdriver, roll of electrical tape, bodcard copies + coxing card/other ID, watch)
- Know your opposition: Who are they? What do their blades look like? Are you on City or Towpath side?
- Check the weather: wear appropriate kit
- Double check timings

# Circulation (Boathouse Island)

- Boat (from downstream rafts) pointing towards the Gut and go down in the TRANSIT LANE. Be aware that there may be crews joining the Transit Lane near the Cherwell cut who have crossed after boating from Univ.
- STAY ABOVE THE GUT
- Spin under marshal instructions and pair up to await racing.
- After the race, return beside the Meadow to landing rafts.

---

# Circulation (Longbridges)

- Boat with your bow ball pointing upstream towards the head.
- Pair up, and proceed upstream towards the start waiting to race.
- After the race, spin at the Head, come down by the Meadows then the boathouses, stay in the TRANSIT LANE, when landing: cross and land with bows pointed downstream.

---

# Circulation (Univ Raft)

- Boat bow ball pointing towards the Gut. Only push off when instructed to by the Univ Marshal. Cross immediately into the TRANSIT LANE, proceed downstream along the Greenbank.
- Crews should be in their boat ready to push off before requesting permission from the Univ Marshal, who will then coordinate with the SU so that you can safely push off after a race comes past.
- Spin under marshal instructions and pair up to await racing.
- After the race, spin at the Head, come down beside the Meadows and then the boathouses in the TRANSIT LANE, until you reach the Marshalling Zone.
- Make it clear to the Marshals/Senior Umpire that you wish to land, and they will instruct you to follow a race in the Towpath Station back to Univ, and land bows upstream.

---

# Circulation (Isis Boathouse)

- Donnington Bridge marks the downstream most point of the Event Managed Area.
- Only Isis Boathouse crews may be downstream of Donnington Bridge.
- Crews downstream of Donnington Bridge must adhere to the OURCs rules for a standard outing.
- Head upstream through the gut, find your opposition, pair up, and continue upstream towards the start.
- After the race, spin at the Head, and continue all the way downstream in the TRANSIT LANE
- Cross to standard circulation as you enter the Gut. Continue in standard circulation and proceed downstream past Donnington Bridge.

# Obeying the rules

- The stagger is set by the Senior Umpire, not by you or your coach
- Side-by-side racing, your whole boat must stay in your own lane and not impede the other crew or transiting boats.
- Don't get in the way of other crews while racing, or indeed when warming up!
- Be on time for your race or risk DSQ or penalties. Note that races are timed in blocks: aim to be at the start line when your block is set to start.
- Any appeals should adhere to the rules (read them!!)
- You may have to prove the identity of your crew and/or cox before racing or when you return: have photos of the ID of every crew member printed out in your pocket

# Race day timing

- 3 Hours Before: make sure crew has eaten
- ??? : meet at lodge OR boat house
- 1 hour before race: Arrive at boathouse.
- 30-40 mins before: hands on (if space on raft)
- 20-25 (max) mins before: push off
- 5 mins to go, be queuing for start and ready
- Race time: Go!

<br>

- Do not be late to the start!

---

# Warming your crew up to race

- Assume there will be no water warmup
- The crew should warm up well on land: there is no guarantee of any time to warm up on water
- Make sure you have practised this beforehand and that your crew gets properly warm
  - high heart rate
  - sweating

---

# Lining up for side-by-side

- Aim for center of lane well before you get to the start line.
- Wind may push you sideways: Go to upwind side of your lane if crosswinds.
- Remember you can back down to straighten as well as row on. Please only use 1 rower at a time to adjust position.
- You may have to wait for river traffic: keep on top of position.
- Staggered start: we will set this.
- We WILL NOT start a race if you are obviously not straight, ready or stagger is not correct so don't panic
- We WILL start a race, regardless of hand up, if you look ready and are not actively adjusting so be ready

---

# Side-by-side start

- Command is "Club A, Club B... Attention... Go!"
- If the stream is strong command will be shorter: listen to the SU for instructions!
- Look down your lane and focus on steering. Don't look directly at the next-door crew (you may veer towards them). Sneak peeks briefly.

# Racing Calls

- You can prep and practice a race plan beforehand:
  - Plan a few technique points to make calls on
  - Use a few landmarks: "passing our raft", "end of boathouses"
  - Think about what would motivate you to pull harder! Ask your crew what they would like to hear. Sound confident: rehearse before hand if you need to.
- Tell your rowers where you are: be honest but sound positive. Anyone can catch a sudden crab! Remember, in novice racing the winner is often the one who DOESN'T make a mistake: see Somerville in the 2022 W final: <https://youtu.be/qIXQKo3uhL8?t=120>

# At the finish line

- Row through the finish line, but then wind down straight away. Don't call 'easy there' or 'hold it' or say 'we've won'.
- 'Straight finish' (No stagger)
- The race is over when your bow ball crosses the clackerboard.
- If you wish to appeal the race: raise your hand at finish
- Listen to marshals and stop as soon as you're asked.
- Spin where you are told: ROWING ON WITH STROKE SIDE and row back close to the Meadows bank.
- Do not lie down in the boat unless you are unwell: you will be mistaken for someone having a medical emergency.
- Make sure the whole crew knows when your next race is before leaving the river.

---

# Landing

- Stay upstream until a raft is free.
- Don't drift: You can back down if needed.
- If landing the wrong way from training:
  - Blades on the raft get out first
  - Then they hold the boat for the others!

---

# Problems?

- If you catch a crab, start rowing again asap: make sure you talk through with your crew what you are going to do if this happens.
- Blade clashes in races may lead to appeals
- Equipment failure discuss with Racedesk ASAP. They may not be able to reschedule though.
- Equipment conflicts (adjacent races) also discuss with Racedesk
- Register ALL SUBSTITUTIONS with Racedesk before you boat
- Racedesk is in the new Merton Small Boat bay (not Longbridges!)
  - For problems on the day, try to go in person: They may not get e-mails

# Final Training Advice

- STICK CLOSELY TO BOWSIDE in training unless overtaking: this is an existing rule. This includes entry to/exit from the Gut. Tuck back in quickly after overtaking. You need to learn to steer very near the bank.
- Practice your warm-up and remember it won't be very long on water for the short course.
- Practice starts, but be careful to do them safely including stopping safely (check behind you first) and not obstructing other crews.
- Practice bursts of pressure, with pushes in.
- Practice the crew catching a crab and what you will call if it happens.
