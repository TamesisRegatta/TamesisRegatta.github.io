## Captains { #captains .tr-new-page }

### What schedule should my racing crews expect? { #schedule }

Unless the event plan is altered due to river or weather conditions, each crew is guaranteed at least two races.
All crews should expect to race in a heat on day one, {{ dates.regatta_start | ukdate }}, which will be a side-by-side race against another crew.

If your crew loses their day one heat, they will compete in the repechage, which is likely to take place the next day.
If your crew wins either a heat or the repechage, you will race on the following days in a basic side-by-side knock-out format.
When the draw is released (hopefully on Nov 18th), you will know when your boat is scheduled to race, and you will be able to follow through the various permutations all the way to the final.

The timetable of racing and the draw itself may need to be changed at short notice, for instance if conditions necessitate reducing the amount of racing on any given day.
We will make every effort to give as much notice of changes as possible.

### Do we have to provide marshals? { #marshals }

Yes, just like other regattas on the Isis organised by OURCs, such as Torpids and Summer VIIIs.
For Tamesis, clubs must provide 1 experienced marshal for each boat entered.
Two boats entered = two marshalling slots.
If you must provide marshals, you will be required to source volunteers from your club who have marshalled previously and register their name with the Race Committee.
They will also be required to attend the pre-regatta marshalling briefing on the evening of {{ dates.marshal_briefing | ukdate }} in the OC Tanner room at Linacre College (or remotely online, if in-person attendance is impossible).
This mandatory pre-regatta briefing will cover the marshalling duties in detail.

There is a Marshal FAQ on the website which we recommend you circulate to your volunteers.
You as a captain or your individually nominated marshals can fill your volunteer slots by filling out a Google form which will be circulated to captains.

### What if we need to make a substitution? { #substitutions }

You can make subs after entries close.
The crew does not have to be the same crew every day.
Substitutions may be made freely within the eligibility rules (e.g. associates are allowed as long as they are still a novice).
You may also enter composite crews with other clubs if you cannot field an eight with novices from your own club.

The race committee must be informed of any substitutions before the crew in question boats, either on the entry system up to the online substitution deadline, or in person at Race Desk on the day of racing.

If a crew races as a friendly crew, for example if you have to substitute a senior rower into your boat on any day, then it shall remain friendly from that point on.

### When do entries close? { #entries-close }

Entries close on {{ dates.entries_close | ukdatetime }}.
This is also the same deadline for having complete and valid crews.
See below for more details about valid crews.

### What is a scratch deadline? { #scratch-deadline }

The scratch deadline is Sunday Nov 15th at midnight.
If you must withdraw, do so before this date.
If you scratch after this date, you will still be charged entry fees and still have to provide marshals based on the number of boats you entered.

### Does everyone need to be swim tested? { #swim-tests }

Yes, your entire crew of rowers and cox must all have a valid swim test.
You can verify the swim tests of your athletes and coxes by checking their profile on the OURCs system to make sure your crew has all been swim tested in the last 6 years.

If members of your crew are booked into a swim test before the regatta begins on the 25th, but after the "complete crews" deadline on the 16th, they might still be eligible.
Please contact the Rowing Sabbatical Officer to confirm eligibility at [ourcs.sabbatical@sport.ox.ac.uk](mailto:ourcs.sabbatical@sport.ox.ac.uk).

### What is a friendly crew vs a competitive crew? { #friendly-competitive }

A competitive crew must consist of one cox and eight novice rowers.

A friendly crew must consist of one cox and a minimum of five novice rowers and up to three non-novice rowers.
A friendly crew will always be considered the losing crew if they have raced against a competitive crew.
If you lose to a friendly crew in a heat, then you will proceed through to the repechage instead of to round one.

### When is the award ceremony? { #awards }

The awards ceremony will be at approximately {{ dates.awards_ceremony.strftime("%H:%M") }} on {{ dates.awards_ceremony | ukdate }} at Boathouse Island Race Desk (the small boats bay next to the Merton/Worcester boathouse).

Coaches are encouraged to attend to celebrate with their crews.

### Does my cox have to bring copies of Bod cards into the boat? { #identification }

Coxes shall be required to be in possession of identification for all crew members while they are on the water.
Such identification must contain a picture of the person being identified.
A print-out or photocopy of a University card (rather than the actual card), or coxing licence will be sufficient.
Crews who fail an identity check will not be allowed to push off, or if the check takes place after racing, may be disqualified.

### What if my coxes can't attend the coxing briefing on Nov 19th? { #coxing-briefing }

In cases of illness or academic clashes, exemptions may be given by the Regatta Captain of Coxes in consultation with OURCs.
Exemptions have to be requested in advance via email to [{{ contact.email }}](mailto:{{ contact.email }}) no later than 11am on the day of the briefing ({{ dates.coxing_briefing | ukdate }}).
Your email should include the cox's name and coxing history (races, coxing status etc).
However, exemptions, if at all, can only be given to coxes who are X or S status.
Coxes who have been exempted from attending the briefing are required to review the relevant information, especially the circulation pattern, which will be made available on the regatta web page and circulated to all boat clubs via email.

### What counts as a novice rower? { #novice }

A rower is considered to be a novice if, prior to {{ dates.novice_cutoff | ukdate }}, they have not competed as a rower (sculling or sweep) in any of the following:

- Any British Rowing (or foreign, regional, or international equivalent) regatta or head race.
- Any other event where they represented their school, college, or university (for example, bumps races including rowing on, Isis Winter League, Clare Novices Regatta, Oriel Regatta, etc.)

For the avoidance of doubt, rowers who learned to row prior to the start of Michaelmas term {{ dates.year }} are eligible as long as they have never competed prior to the start of Michaelmas term {{ dates.year }}.
